Acoustic design

Sound,
by design.

Tuning isn't an EQ preset added at the end. It starts with the diaphragm material, the chamber volume and the size of one vent. Here is what each decision does to what you hear.

The driver

40 mm, 0.3 g.

Liquid-crystal polymer reinforced with carbon fibre is stiffer than PET and lighter than aluminium. A stiff, light cone moves as one piston for longer before it starts to flex, and flex is where distortion comes from.

Twenty to twenty thousand.

Band by band, low to high

01 · 20 – 80 Hz

Sub-bass

Felt more than heard.

The rear vent is tuned to the driver's 28 Hz resonance, so the bottom octave stays flat instead of being boosted with EQ. Kick drums arrive as pressure, not boom.

+4 dB

shelf below 100 Hz, matched to how bass is felt

02 · 80 – 250 Hz

Bass

Fast, then gone.

The stiff LCP cone stops moving the moment the signal does, so bass lines keep their pitch and don't blur into the mids. Decay to −30 dB happens in under 4 ms at 100 Hz.

< 4 ms

decay to −30 dB at 100 Hz

03 · 250 Hz – 2 kHz

Mids

Where voices live.

Held within ±1 dB of the reference. Vocals, guitars and dialogue sit where the recording put them, with no scooped mids to fake excitement.

±1 dB

deviation from target, 250 Hz – 2 kHz

04 · 2 – 6 kHz

Presence

Detail without the bite.

A gentle 2 dB dip at 3.5 kHz takes the edge off the ear canal's own resonance. Consonants stay crisp and sibilance stays polite at hour six.

−2 dB

at 3.5 kHz, ear-gain compensation

05 · 6 – 20 kHz

Air

Room to breathe.

The diaphragm's first break-up mode sits above 22 kHz, outside the audible band, so cymbals decay cleanly instead of ringing.

> 22 kHz

first diaphragm break-up mode

Target curve

Tuned to a curve, not a mood.

The reference is a diffuse-field target with a mild low-shelf, which listening panels consistently rate as natural. Drift One follows it within ±1.5 dB from 50 Hz to 8 kHz.

Soundstage

In front of you, not inside your head.

Each driver is angled 8° forward inside the cup, so sound reaches your ear the way a pair of speakers would, from slightly ahead. The image moves out of the middle of your skull and onto a stage in front of you.
